# DM Playbook v2.0 — Eliminate the Nine Steps, Don't Answer Them

## Part 0 — The reframe (why v1.0 wasn't good enough)

Rich's critique of v1.0: the messages still read like every other business
trying to get someone to engage, and a cold reader must survive nine mental
steps (read it → finish it → decide it's worth replying → "what are they
selling?" → "do I have an urgent problem?" → "why am I reading this?" →
"what I do already works" → "do I have the energy?" → "I've tried this
before and it sucked") before anything happens.

The finding from the literature: **the top performers don't answer those
nine objections better. They design first touches where most of the steps
never occur.** Three structural moves do almost all the work:

1. **The message IS the value.** Reading it delivers the product, before
   any decision to engage. Steps 3-8 collapse because nothing is being
   asked yet.
2. **The timing IS the relevance.** Message only when a live, dated event
   exists in the prospect's world. Step 5 ("do I have an urgent problem?")
   is answered by the event, not by copy.
3. **Most first touches shouldn't be cold at all.** The highest-converting
   paths (referral, prior familiarity, inbound from published proof) skip
   all nine steps. Cold DM volume is the tax you pay for not engineering
   these.

One honest caveat before the methods: rigorous peer-reviewed research on
cold DMs specifically barely exists. What exists is (a) peer-reviewed
psychology of persuasion and compliance, (b) large-sample vendor datasets
(millions of emails, self-interested but real), and (c) practitioner
consensus. Each method below is labeled accordingly. Nothing is invented.

## Part 1 — The 15 methods (what the top 0.01% do differently)

**M1. Give the lead away in the first message.**
Deliver one real, dated, niche-relevant lead with no gate and no ask.
Reciprocity is among the most replicated influence findings (Regan 1971,
the classic experiment; Cialdini's *Influence* synthesizes decades of
replication) [peer-reviewed]. For you it is uniquely cheap: your product
IS a message-sized artifact. Nobody else's sample fits in a DM.

**M2. Trigger-event timing.**
Message within days of a public event in THEIR niche (a CFO resignation
with no successor, a WARN notice, a funding round). Practitioner doctrine
(Craig Elias, *Shift*: trigger-event selling) [practitioner], but for you
it's self-executing: your scanner generates the trigger list. The rule:
no live trigger in their niche = they stay in the queue. You never send a
generic opener again.

**M3. Radical brevity.**
Boomerang's analysis of 40M+ emails: 50-125 words got the highest
response rates; response drops steeply past that
([Boomerang blog](https://blog.boomerangapp.com/2016/02/7-tips-for-getting-more-responses-to-your-emails-with-data/),
[HubSpot summary](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/ideal-length-sales-email))
[large-sample vendor]. LinkedIn's own InMail data: shorter InMails
significantly outperform, and messages under ~400 characters do best
([LinkedIn Talent Blog](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-strategy/these-inmails-get-best-response-rates))
[platform data]. v2.0 cap: 45 words / ~350 characters for openers.

**M4. Write at a 3rd-grade reading level.**
Same Boomerang dataset: 3rd-grade reading level beat college-level
writing by ~36% on response [large-sample vendor]. Not dumbing down;
removing abstraction. "I find searches before they post" beats "I surface
pre-mandate hiring signals."

**M5. Interest-based CTA, never a meeting ask.**
Gong's cold-email analysis: asking for INTEREST ("worth a look?")
roughly doubles meeting-booked rates vs. asking for a specific meeting
time ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/this-surprising-cold-email-cta-will-help-you-book-a-lot-more-meetings));
a separate 304K-email analysis reached the same conclusion
([GrowLeads study](https://growleads.io/blog/interest-based-ctas-vs-meeting-requests-study/))
[large-sample vendor]. Your CTA ceiling is one low-friction question
answerable in one word.

**M6. Teach a reframe (Challenger).**
CEB's study of 6,000+ salespeople (Dixon & Adamson, *The Challenger
Sale*): the highest performers in complex sales win by teaching the
customer something new about THEIR business, not by relationship warmth
alone [book-published research on a large sample]. Your reframe: "the
search you lose was decided before the posting existed." Deliver it as
evidence (M1), never as a slogan.

**M7. Ask for advice, sincerely.**
Brooks, Gino & Schweitzer (2015, *Management Science*): people who seek
advice are judged MORE competent by the person asked, not less
([paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2014.2054))
[peer-reviewed]. This validates the research-frame openers (Jennifer,
Mary). Kept, and extended: the advice ask must be specific enough that
answering it is intrinsically interesting to an expert.

**M8. Engineer referrals instead of scaling cold.**
Schmitt, Skiera & Van den Bulte (2011, *Journal of Marketing*): referred
customers show higher margins, higher retention, and ~16-25% higher
lifetime value across ~10,000 customers
([paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jm.75.1.46))
[peer-reviewed]. The 0.01% move: every informant call, every demo
feedback session, every pilot ends with a NAMED ask ("who is one
recruiter in [niche] who'd want this?"), and the referral message leads
with the mutual's name. Your Rich W. / Jeff / Sylvie network is
underexploited on this axis.

**M9. Be familiar before you arrive (mere exposure).**
Zajonc (1968): repeated exposure alone increases liking [peer-reviewed].
Practice: comment substantively on a prospect's posts 1-2 weeks before
any DM; they've seen your name twice before you ask for anything
[practitioner application of peer-reviewed effect]. Ten thoughtful
comments a week is a system, not a hope.

**M10. The transparency disarm (pratfall).**
Aronson, Willerman & Floyd (1966): a competent person who admits a small
flaw becomes MORE likable [peer-reviewed]. Practice: name the elephant.
"You don't know me and this is a cold message" read honestly outperforms
pretending it isn't one [practitioner: Josh Braun's permission-based
openers]. Step 4 of Rich's nine ("what are they selling me?") dies here:
tell them, immediately, in plain words.

**M11. Explicit freedom to refuse ("but you are free").**
Guéguen & Pascual (2000) and Carpenter's meta-analysis (2013, 42 studies,
~22,000 participants): adding an explicit "you're free to say no"
roughly DOUBLES compliance with small requests [peer-reviewed,
meta-analyzed; one of the strongest effects in the compliance
literature]. One closing line: "If this isn't useful, ignoring it is a
fine answer." This kills step 8 (energy) and reactance at once.

**M12. Personalization that proves work, not flattery.**
Backlinko/Pitchbox, 12M outreach emails: personalized messages get
~30%+ higher reply rates, but only 8.5% of all outreach gets ANY reply
([study](https://backlinko.com/email-outreach-study))
[large-sample vendor]. The 0.01% personalize the SUBSTANCE (their niche,
their open searches, their market's live trigger), not the surface
(alma mater, recent post). M1+M2 do this automatically.

**M13. Honest information gaps.**
Loewenstein (1994): curiosity spikes when a specific, resolvable gap is
exposed [peer-reviewed theory]. Your metered-brief mechanic already is
this ("decision-maker identified, named in pilot briefs"). In DMs: name
the fact you hold ("I'm holding one in med-device right now") without
clickbait. The gap must resolve honestly the moment they reply.

**M14. Zero-effort reply design.**
Every message ends so that a one-word reply advances the conversation
("Worth sending?" "CFO or CHRO?" "Yes or no is fine"). Consistent with
Gong's finding that low-friction interest CTAs work even on executives
([Gong exec-reply data](https://www.gong.io/blog/do-execs-really-reply-to-cold-email-here-s-what-the-data-says))
[vendor data]. Rich's step 8 is an energy budget; price the reply at one
word.

**M15. Publish the proof so they come to you.**
The only channel where ALL nine steps vanish is inbound. Post one
anonymized hit per week ("flagged this CFO search 6 weeks before the
posting; here were the signs") on LinkedIn. Practitioner consensus
[lowest evidence tier, labeled as such], but it compounds your existing
inbound form, and every M1 lead you give away is simultaneously content.
The 0.01% are discovered more than they outreach.

## Part 2 — The rewritten library

Rules inherited: no em dashes, D9 (no numeric confidence), lane metering,
collision ledger. New caps: openers ≤45 words, one one-word-answerable
question, trigger required.

**N1. Cold opener, boutique recruiter (M1+M2+M5+M14). THE workhorse.**
> [Company] looks ready to open a CFO search. Their CFO resigned June 12,
> no successor named, interim confirmed in Friday's filing.
>
> Yours, no catch. I find these before they post. Want the next one in
> your niche?

*(The lead is real, from the scanner, collision-ledger checked, L1 rules:
no contacts. If no live trigger exists in their niche, they don't get
messaged. 38 words.)*

**N2. Cold opener, transparency variant (M10+M11) for pattern-saturated
prospects (recruiting-tech-fatigued, senior).**
> Cold message, so here's the whole pitch in one line: I predict which
> companies will open senior searches before anything posts, and I'm
> holding one in [niche] right now.
>
> Want it? If not, ignoring this is a fine answer.

**N3. Big-firm informant (M7, validated by Brooks et al.).**
> You've watched a hundred searches start from the inside. I'm building
> the outside-in version and one thing keeps puzzling me: how often does
> the winning firm already have the relationship before the opening
> officially exists?
>
> Thirty seconds of your read would genuinely help.

**N4. Coach opener. UNCHANGED from v1.0 (Jennifer).** It already follows
M7 and has no ask. Add the M11 line only if it goes to a stranger:
> When a client goes quiet between sessions mid-search, what usually
> turns out to be the reason? I'm asking every coach I meet. Feel free
> to skip this if it's a busy week.

**N5. Profile visitor (M10+M13).**
> You looked at my profile, so here's the honest version: I predict
> senior searches before they post. I'm holding one in [their niche]
> from this week.
>
> Want it? One word is fine.

**N6. Referral opener (M8). Highest-priority message in the playbook.**
> [Mutual] said you're the person to show this to. [Company] looks ready
> to open a [role] search: [one dated sign]. That's the product, in one
> message.
>
> Worth sending the next one?

**Follow-up rule change (all threads): every follow-up contains a NEW
dated lead or fact. "Bumping this" and "any thoughts?" are banned. If the
scanner hasn't produced a new trigger for their niche by day 5, the
follow-up waits for one. Day-7 close and 3-touch cap stand (v1.0 R5).**

**Follow-up example (day 3-7, silence):**
> Different company, same pattern: [Company Y], [dated sign], likely
> [role] search forming. Two for free is my limit, so this is the last
> unsolicited one.
>
> If they're landing, say the word and I'll aim them at your actual spec.

*(M1 twice + honest scarcity + M5 interest CTA. The metering rule from
R7 does the closing for you.)*

## Part 3 — The nine steps, and which method kills each

| Rich's step | Killed by |
|---|---|
| 1. Read it | M3 brevity: 2 lines on mobile, no scroll |
| 2. Finish it | M4 reading level + the lead in line one |
| 3. Worth responding? | M14: reply costs one word |
| 4. What are they selling? | M10: stated plainly, immediately |
| 5. Urgent problem? | M2: the trigger IS the urgency, in their niche, dated |
| 6. Why am I reading this? | M1: because it just handed you a lead |
| 7. What I do works | M6 reframe delivered as evidence: here's a search forming that your current method hasn't surfaced |
| 8. Energy to engage? | M11 freedom-to-refuse + M14 one-word reply |
| 9. Tried before, sucked | M1: value arrived BEFORE any commitment, which none of the previous ones did |

Structural methods (M8 referrals, M9 familiarity, M15 publishing) reduce
how often anyone starts at step 1 cold at all. Weekly quota suggestion:
10 substantive comments (M9), 2 referral asks (M8), 1 published hit
(M15), and N1/N6 sends capped by live triggers, not by ambition.

## Part 3b — The price ladder (RATIFIED 2026-08-03, single source of truth)

Free metered sample (lane rules apply: one contact shown, two named and
withheld) → **$750 tailored three-company sample brief** (the L2 offer,
MSPS-002; founder-reviewed, dated public evidence) → **$1,000/month per
mandate** (one active search per mandate; buy more mandates for more
searches; the $750 credits toward the first mandate month if they
continue). Conversation answer to "how much?":

> The tailored sample is $750: three companies in your niche, named
> decision-makers, dated evidence, built for you. If it earns it, the
> subscription is $1,000 a month per mandate, and the $750 credits to
> your first month.
>
> Before price though: what would one placement sourced two months early
> be worth to you?

Never quote any other number. "Seat" is banned vocabulary; the unit is
the mandate.

## Part 4 — Retained from v1.0

R3 brand-per-lane, R4 mechanics (minus the 60-word cap, now 45), R5
cadence + 3-touch cap, R7 metered-brief filter, R8 log-everything, R10
reinforce-after-yes. R1/R2 are superseded by Part 3's step-elimination
frame. The 10v10 discipline applies: run N1 against the old A opener on
matched prospects before declaring victory; the literature above is
evidence, but your niche's measured reply rate is the only verdict that
counts (R8).
