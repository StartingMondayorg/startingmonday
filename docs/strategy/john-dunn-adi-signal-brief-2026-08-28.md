## Why this account now

The supplied prospect report identifies an ADI standalone-company transition after the Resideo separation. The central commercial question is not whether ADI needs more technology products. It is how quickly the company can establish the operating foundation, ownership, and partner model required to run independently while continuing its digital and distribution growth. The separation mechanics and timing still need canonical filing verification.

This is a strong Presidio conversation because the likely work spans network, security, cloud, identity, collaboration, and managed operations. It also has a defined technology owner: James Olender, ADI's SVP and CIO, whose public biography describes both responsibility for ADI's global IT organization and prior leadership through a GE Vernova Wind corporate separation.

## The working hypothesis

The public record suggests that ADI may be balancing three pressures:

1. Establishing a standalone technology foundation without slowing the business's distribution and digital growth.
2. Deciding which network, security, identity, cloud, collaboration, and service-desk capabilities should be built, inherited, or outsourced.
3. Making those decisions against a transition timetable and an operating-cost narrative that has not yet been fully verified from primary filings here.

This is a hypothesis to test with the technology owner, not a diagnosis to announce.

## Presidio relevance

Presidio should enter as an execution and sequencing partner, not as a product catalog:

- **Network:** map the branch and distribution footprint, connectivity dependencies, and the standalone operating standard.
- **Security:** establish the identity, edge, endpoint, monitoring, and governance baseline required for an independent public company.
- **Cloud and applications:** clarify which hosting and enterprise-application dependencies remain shared and which must move.
- **Managed operations:** test whether a co-managed network/security model gives ADI capacity without adding permanent headcount during transition.
- **Channel alignment:** confirm any existing ADI distributor relationship before proposing OEM or integrator involvement. ADI sells technology products itself, so the conversation should focus on corporate IT execution rather than product resale.

## First conversation

The first meeting should be with James Olender or the infrastructure/security leader under his organization. The objective is to learn the actual workstream map, not to present a general capabilities overview.

Suggested opening:

> ADI is scaling its platform and channel while establishing the technology foundation for an independent company. We would like to understand which parts of that foundation are already decided, where the transition creates the most execution risk, and whether a short separation-readiness working session would be useful.

## Discovery questions

### Situation

1. Which technology services and transition dates are currently confirmed for the standalone company?
2. Which leaders own network, security, identity, collaboration, and hosting decisions today?

### Problem

1. Where is the transition creating the most operational or security pressure?
2. Which shared-service dependency is hardest to exit on the desired timeline?
3. Where does the team lack capacity to build and run the standalone foundation?

### Implication

1. What would a delayed technology transition cost against the operating plan?
2. Which customer, branch, or distribution operation is most exposed if a shared service changes before its replacement is ready?

### Need-payoff

1. If the highest-risk workstream were sequenced and staffed now, what would the team protect first?
2. What would a predictable run model allow the internal IT organization to focus on?
